## Summary. The strongest case for the thesis in this repository

The primary verdict is not the weak point and I do not challenge it: at the registered floor the model did not beat a coin, and the authors themselves label the result an "underpowered unresolved, not a tight null" (evaluation.md:139-141) and say the question is "closed **by operator decision, not by evidence**" (REGISTRATION.md:709-710). The weak point is the *causal story* the closure tells about why the design was underpowered. Four documents state as measured fact that the model was "leaning systematically against" momentum; recomputation from the stored rankings and sealed returns gives corr(model, mom_12_1) = **+0.1182** (n = 404), not a correlation near minus one — the contrast sd of 0.36339 comes from per-series dispersion 35 to 58 percent above the assumed null sd, i.e. from a registration-stage modelling assumption, not from model behaviour. Around that error sit three closures that hold less firmly than they read: a registered descriptive split that was never delivered and whose omission the B.2 record nowhere states, a registered reopen schedule whose 221-day trigger the closure's own 1,700-day sizing has made stale, and a closure cost section that prices exactly one route to a powered result and calls it unavailable while never carrying the price of the `low`-effort variant the registration itself named — about 16 to 21 USD on the ledger's own rates, five to seven times the 3.0622 USD left under the cap, and some 37 percent above the registration's own now-stale 11.7812 USD projection.

The whole B.2 computation reproduces exactly from stored primitives (every figure in `reports/stage_b2/evaluation.md`, to four decimals), so the disagreements below are about interpretation and completeness, never about arithmetic in the pipeline.

Recomputation scripts and outputs: `scratchpad/work/agentA/recompute.py`, `recompute.out`, `supp.py`, `supp.out` (under the work directory named in COMMON_RULES).
