## Summary — the strongest case for the thesis in this repository

This is a disciplined repository: pre-registration is honoured, corrections move against the project's own interest, and most closures survive everything Agent A could bring. What does not survive is the *reach* of four of them. **H1** is closed cost-bound with a registered reopening threshold of "A round-trip cost below roughly **3 bps**", and the register states as a general fact that "sub-3 bps round trip needs **$10M–$250M of trailing 30-day volume**" (HYPOTHESES.md:516-517) — while the same file's own fee table records Hyperliquid perps base tier at **3.0 bps round trip** with no volume requirement (HYPOTHESES.md:307) and calls it "**The cheapest venue reachable from British Columbia is Hyperliquid at 3 bps**" (HYPOTHESES.md:322-323); the project recorded 13 days of that venue and never ran the directional pipeline on it. **H11's class B** — 7 of 8 cells positive, the class ADR-042 concedes "would have shown the freshest signal in the study" — was handicapped at **75× its own pre-registered lag** (registered +1 day, applied +75) on the end-of-file date of a *single* snapshot in which `PriceUSD` stops on the same day, and that stage's FAIL ended the project's alpha search by binding decision. **H10** declined the venue's own liquidation label as "weeks of querying … per fill-participant against 2.64M fills" when the recorded week holds **62,905 distinct participant addresses** (measured) — about 19 hours of free, keyless, per-address querying. And "A **negative bounded D.1 closes the thin tail (and H3) completely**" (HYPOTHESES.md:205) rests on two instruments, while the two largest measured captures in the whole census — **NOT +31.15** and **TNSR +35.50 bps**, both clearing every bar but one clause of the capacity tier — were never put through the fill model that produced the closure.
